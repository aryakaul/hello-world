## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment: content-specific, composition-generic.** Strip the words and what remains is a stock directory template — page title, full-width search with absolutely-positioned dropdown, hero canvas, `auto-fill minmax(9.5rem)` card grid, gray hairline footer. That skeleton would serve a park finder or a conference schedule unedited. `styles.css` has zero media queries, zero `:focus`, zero transitions, zero `prefers-color-scheme`, zero `prefers-reduced-motion`. Links are `#0a58ca` — browser blue, nudged. For a product whose second principle is "Warmth is the point," the visual language is a beige spreadsheet: the phrase set is toasts and compliments, the rendering is a tax table.

Three elements are genuinely authored: the row anatomy (respelling heaviest and widest, English demoted to 0.9rem gray — the correct hierarchy for say-it-out-loud, and the opposite of what phrasebooks do), the `statusBadge()` refusal that structurally cannot emit "verified" without a real handle, and the per-language `guidance` line ("Official; use everywhere" / "Largest first language; Oromia region") which answers the question a traveler actually has and which no translation app answers.

The layout also contradicts the stated positioning: PRODUCT.md says country-first, but home spends ~2,900 of its 3,404 mobile pixels on a flat alphabetical list of 68 **languages** and contains no list of the 49 **countries** at all.

**Deterministic scan: 0 findings, and that number is close to meaningless.** `detect.mjs --json index.html` returned `[]`, exit 0 — but `index.html` is a 15-line shell (`<main id="app" aria-live="polite">` plus two script tags) and the entire UI is painted client-side by `app.js`. A second pass across `styles.css`, `app.js`, and `globe.js` also returned `[]`. The rendered-DOM scan could not run: URL mode requires puppeteer, which is not installed on this machine, and the file-dump fallback was blocked by the read-only constraint in force at the time. **Treat the clean scan as "not measured", not "clean."** Every real finding below came from browser measurement or source reading.

**Visual overlays: not available.** Script injection was never attempted — it requires `live-server.mjs`, which plan mode forbade. No user-visible overlay exists in any browser tab; the evidence is the measurement tables instead.

## Overall Impression

The data layer is the most disciplined part of this project by a wide margin — 1330 entries, evidence-based verification methodology, a validator that makes dishonest attribution structurally impossible. The interface has not received a fraction of that rigor, and it is now the weakest link in a product whose whole premise is speed at a socially high-stakes moment.

Two defects make the primary path actively unreliable rather than merely rough: search results cannot be reached by keyboard at all, and pressing Enter — the fastest path, the one the product is designed around — silently opens the wrong country for common queries.

The single biggest opportunity is the moment before speaking. Right now the last three things a user reads before opening their mouth are an UNVERIFIED badge, "suggest a fix", and "I speak this — verify it." The design's final word is doubt, twice, plus two requests for free labor. That is an honest product expressing its honesty in exactly the way that undermines its own use case.

## What's Working

1. **Respelling-as-hero row anatomy** (`phraseTable`, app.js:274-334). English demoted to 0.9rem `#555`, native script mid-row, anglicized respelling carrying the weight and the widest column. A real judgment about saying rather than reading, and the inverse of what most phrasebooks do.
2. **Provenance enforced in code, not in copy** (`statusBadge`, app.js:216-238). Cannot emit "verified" without `verified_by.handle`, and it validates the handle against `/^[A-Za-z0-9-]+$/`. A principle that cannot rot because the render path won't permit it.
3. **The `guidance` line** under each language heading. One line, right place, right size, answering "is it appropriate to use this with the person in front of me." The most product-specific pixel on the site.

Structurally sound, worth preserving: `navGen` race-guarding is disciplined and correct throughout `app.js`; `.globe-slot { min-height: 20rem }` reserves space and prevents layout shift; zero console errors across every route tested; zero horizontal overflow at 390px and 320px, including Devanagari, Ethiopic, and Sinhala pages.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] Search results are unreachable by keyboard
**What:** `search.addEventListener("blur", …setTimeout(…150))` at app.js:184-188 hides the dropdown 150ms after blur regardless of where focus went. The `pointerdown`/`preventDefault` guard at line 180 protects the mouse only. Tab toward a result and it vanishes before you arrive; ArrowDown does nothing, so no alternative path exists.
**Why it matters:** the primary navigation of the product is 100% unavailable to keyboard and switch users. Not degraded — closed.
**Fix:** replace `blur`+timeout with `focusout` gated on `searchWrap.contains(event.relatedTarget)`; add ArrowUp/ArrowDown roving focus with `role="combobox"` / `role="listbox"` / `aria-activedescendant`.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P0] Enter navigates to the wrong country
**What:** `fillResults` (app.js:129-143) matches the query anywhere inside `c.name + " " + c.languages.join(" ")` via bare `includes()`, emitting countries in index order; Enter clicks the first anchor (app.js:169-175). Confirmed in source and live: `port` → **Brazil**, `usa` → **Nigeria** (H**ausa**), `in` → Argentina, China, Ethiopia before India.
**Why it matters:** type three letters, hit Enter, hold out the phone — the intended fastest path — is silently wrong, with nothing on screen explaining why Brazil appeared.
**Fix:** rank exact name match > name prefix > name substring > language match; show the match reason on language-derived rows ("Brazil — Portuguese"); bold the matched substring; require a name match at the top before Enter selects.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P1] Contribution out-shouts the phrase, breaking a stated invariant
**What:** every row emits one badge plus two link-blue CTAs, inside the same table cell as the respelling — 128 badges and 238 `.entry-action` links on `#/india`. Each CTA measures 72×18px, with two stacked 12px apart.
**Why it matters:** PRODUCT.md states contribution "never competes with the lookup path for the primary user's attention." Measured at 390px, the blue links occupy more vertical space than the phrases they annotate. The invariant is already violated.
**Fix:** collapse to one "found a mistake on this page? →" affordance per language section, or an overflow/long-press per row. If kept inline, they belong in a de-emphasized `#666` micro-row, never link-blue.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable distill`

### [P1] No reassurance and no legend at the moment of speaking
**What:** 128 identical UNVERIFIED badges on one page convey zero information while 100% of the corpus is `status: "ai"` (measured: 0 verified entries across all 68 language files). The CAPS-stress convention is documented only in README.md. Gender/register notes ("Female: chalti hoon") render at 0.8rem `#666` *below* the badge and both CTAs — the faintest text in the row is the fact that decides whether the user is understood or embarrassed.
**Why it matters:** this is the emotional valley at the exact instant the product exists to serve. The design amplifies doubt and suppresses the two facts that make a phrase land.
**Fix:** replace per-row badges with one honest page-level statement ("All 1330 entries are AI drafts cross-checked against Google Translate; no native speaker has reviewed them yet"), reserving the badge for when rows actually differ. Add a one-line legend above every table ("CAPS = stress this syllable"). Promote register/gender notes above the badge row with real weight.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

### [P2] The globe is invisible to assistive tech and to sunlight
**What:** `.globe-canvas` has no `role`, no `aria-label`, no fallback children, `tabIndex === -1` — it does not appear in the accessibility tree at all, and 43% of the mobile fold is void to a screen reader. Coverage is encoded by hue alone: covered `#86efac` against land `#e7e5e4` is **1.12:1**; ocean against land is **1.03:1**.
**Why it matters:** PRODUCT.md's operating context is "one-handed, at arm's length, often outdoors." Under glare the globe collapses into a uniform pale disc and "tap a green country" becomes unfollowable.
**Fix:** give covered countries a luminance and stroke difference, not just hue; label the canvas `role="img"` with an `aria-label` summarizing coverage; add a real keyboard-reachable country list on home as its text equivalent — which also fixes the missing country-first entry point.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable colorize`

### [P2] UI-component boundaries fail non-text contrast
**What:** measured against `#fafaf7` — search input border `#bbb` **1.84:1**, language pill border `#ddd` **1.30:1**, results dropdown border `#ddd` **1.30:1**, audio button border `#c9dcff` **1.32:1**. All fail WCAG 1.4.11's 3:1 for UI components. Text failures alongside them: `.context` `#999` at **2.72:1** (this is the disambiguating usage guidance), footer disclaimer `#777` at **4.28:1**, the UNVERIFIED badge at **4.43:1**, globe toast text at **4.43:1**.
**Why it matters:** the search box — the primary control — has a border almost invisible outdoors, and `.context` carries the text that distinguishes near-identical phrases.
**Fix:** darken component borders to at least 3:1 and lift `.context` to `#666` or better. Note the badge and footer miss by 0.07 and 0.22 respectively — trivially fixable, not visually dramatic.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable polish`

### [P3] Long country pages have no wayfinding and no loading feedback
**What:** `#/india` measures **24,124px at 390px wide — 28.6 screens** — across 7 language sections with 247 focusable elements, fetched sequentially while the previous page stays on screen. No sticky headings, no jump nav. Scrolled into the middle you cannot tell whether the phrase you are about to say is Hindi, Tamil, or Bengali.
**Fix:** sticky language header, a language chip row at the top of country pages, `Promise.all` fetching with a visible loading state.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout`

## Cognitive Load

**7 of 8 checks fail — critical.** Single focus fails (three coequal nav systems on home). Chunking fails (68 undifferentiated pills; 17 phrases in one flat table with no greetings/thanks/toasts grouping). Grouping fails (slang dashed rules sit at different y-positions across columns, so which script pairs with which respelling is guesswork). One-thing-at-a-time fails (28.6 screens). ≤4 options fails. Working memory fails (no sticky language header). Progressive disclosure fails (nothing deferred). Only visual hierarchy partially passes, on the strength of the respelling.

Decision points exceeding four visible options: home language grid (**68**), search dropdown for `p` (**12+**, all rendered), `#/india` language sections (**7**), "Spoken in:" on `#/lang/spanish` (**7** inline 17px links).

## Emotional Journey

**Opening (earned peak):** "hello, world" over a green-flecked globe is charming and confident — the best moment in the product.

**Valley 1, the search betrayal:** `port` → Brazil, `usa` → Nigeria, `deutsch` → "No matches." No match highlighting, no "matched via Portuguese." It reads as *the product doesn't understand me*, at the second you can least afford it.

**Valley 2, the unmanaged high-stakes instant:** eye contact made, you look down at `BWEH-nohs DEE-ahs` wrapped across two lines mid-phrase, and directly beneath it sit a yellow UNVERIFIED badge, "suggest a fix", and "I speak this — verify it." Nothing says "close enough to be understood." Nothing explains the CAPS. Honesty is principle #3 and it is right, but this expression of it converts honesty into stage fright.

**Peak-end failure:** every page ends on a gray `#777` disclaimer that everything is AI-generated and awaiting review. The product opens with warmth and closes with a legal notice. No "go say it," no return of the opening tone. Warm start, cold middle, colder finish — and the dead ends ("Page not found.", "No matches.", a toast that vanishes in 2.2s) are affectless in a product whose subject is warmth.

## Persona Red Flags

**Casey (distracted mobile, one-handed):** `input.search` sits at **y=68** — top 8% of the screen, outside the one-handed thumb arc, on a product whose defining posture is one-handed. `.back-link` at **y=16** is the only in-page escape, in the hardest corner to reach. Measured sub-44px targets: `.entry-action` 72×18, language heading links 106×22.5, footer GitHub link 220×16, "Add it →" 227×17. Globe: Belgium and Switzerland render ~5px wide, `nearestCovered` snaps within 14px so near-misses become *wrong countries*, `endPointer` cancels a tap if the pointer moves ≥5px (a thumb on a moving bus never holds that), and `touch-action: none` means an upward scroll swipe spins the earth. Interruption costs everything: any navigation triggers `scrollTo(0,0)` plus full re-render, so returning from a wrong country leaves the search box empty and the query gone.

**Sam (screen reader, keyboard-only):** the primary path is closed (P0). `<main id="app" aria-live="polite">` wraps the entire app and every route change replaces the whole subtree, so navigating to India queues **15,423 characters** as a single polite announcement — aria-live misuse that is worse than no announcement. `document.title` is constant across all routes, so history entries are indistinguishable. The globe contributes nothing to the accessibility tree. Phrase tables have **0 `<th>` and 0 `[scope]`** — columns are unlabelled. **0 of 119 `.native` cells carry a `lang` attribute**, and no element anywhere carries `dir`, including Urdu, Arabic, Hebrew, and Persian rendered inside an LTR document. On `#/india`, 119 links read exactly "suggest a fix" and 119 read exactly "I speak this — verify it," undifferentiated. No skip link, no `<nav>`, `<header>` and `<footer>` nested inside `<main>` so neither exposes a landmark. Focus rings do exist — the UA default at 5.86:1, no suppression found — but no custom style, so the ring traces a 72×18 box on the contributor links and is untested against white pills on `#fafaf7`.

**Priya (traveler mid-conversation, derived from PRODUCT.md):** types `gracias` → "No matches." Phrase search is unsupported and the empty state never says what *is* supported. Reaches `#/mexico` and must scroll five-plus screens to "Thank you" — fixed phrase order, no jump nav, no per-phrase deep link, stranger still standing there. The row she needs shows two Spanish lines whose dashed separators don't align across columns, so she cannot tell at a glance which is slang. She wants to hand the phone over so the stranger can read the native script — `.native` is a 30% column at 1.05rem that wraps "Buenos / días", and there is no hand-over or large-text mode. No audio exists (`index.json` has no `audio` key, so `audioButton` never renders), so everything rests on a respelling whose CAPS convention is never explained on screen.

## Minor Observations

- Search input is 16px while body is 18px — the primary control has smaller text than body copy. Keep 16px (it correctly prevents iOS zoom-on-focus) and fix the relationship instead.
- **No service worker, no manifest, no `theme-color`.** PRODUCT.md says "works fully offline," but that holds only for runtime requests — a cold load with no signal fails outright. The claim and the implementation do not currently meet.
- The globe stack is **168KB of the ~282KB page — 60% of the payload** — loaded sequentially via chained promises on the home route, i.e. the heaviest payload serves the slowest path to the task.
- `.audio-btn` is styled at 32×32, below 44, and will matter the moment audio ships. Same for the dead `.badge-verified` path.
- The not-found page has zero headings.
- `.globe-hint` wraps to two lines at 390px, orphaning "country"; on `#/lang/spanish`, "🇨🇺 Cuba" wraps between flag and name.
- No `enterkeyhint="search"` on the input. No `:hover` on `.lang-pill` despite 68 of them.
- Assessment A reported a `favicon.ico` 404; Assessment B's console capture recorded zero messages across every route. Both are consistent — a favicon 404 is a network failure that Chromium does not always surface to the console buffer. Cosmetic either way.
- Discounted as a false positive: an apparent deep-link routing bug (`#/ethiopia` bouncing to home) was an artifact of both agents briefly sharing one browser tab. Re-verified in isolation — deep-linking works correctly on cold load. `navGen` guarding is sound.

## Questions to Consider

1. If 100% of 1330 entries are `status: "ai"`, what does printing UNVERIFIED 128 times on one page tell anyone — and what would you lose by saying it once, loudly, at the top?
2. Positioning says country-first. Why is there no list of the 49 countries anywhere on home, while 68 languages take 85% of the mobile scroll?
3. The globe is the largest object on the surface and the slowest route to the task. If you deleted it, would the primary user be worse off — or just less charmed?
4. The last thing a user reads before speaking to a stranger is a doubt badge and two requests for free labor. What should it be instead?
5. "The moment is the constraint." What is the real second-count from cold load to "Gracias" on screen, measured on a phone, outdoors, on a bad connection?
6. If the stranger takes the phone to read the native script, what does the design do differently? Currently: nothing.
7. Warmth is principle #2. Name one pixel on this site that is warm.
